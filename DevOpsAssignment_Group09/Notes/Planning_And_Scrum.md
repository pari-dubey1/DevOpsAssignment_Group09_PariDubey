# Agile Methodology and Scrum

---

## 1. Agile Methodology

### 1.1 Introduction

**Agile Methodology** is a software development approach in which software is developed in **small, frequent iterations** rather than developing the complete software at once.

Each iteration is called a **Sprint** and usually produces a working part of the software.

### Agile focuses on

* Continuous development and improvement
* Customer satisfaction
* Frequent delivery of working software
* Team collaboration
* Quick response to changing requirements
* Continuous feedback

### Example

Suppose we are developing an **Online Shopping Website**.

Instead of developing the entire website at once:

| Sprint   | Features             |
| -------- | -------------------- |
| Sprint 1 | Login & Registration |
| Sprint 2 | Product Catalogue    |
| Sprint 3 | Shopping Cart        |
| Sprint 4 | Payment              |
| Sprint 5 | Order Tracking       |

Thus, the customer receives useful features regularly.

---

## 1.2 Agile Manifesto

The **Agile Manifesto** was created in **2001** to provide values for better software development.

There are **4 main values**:

### 1. Individuals and Interactions Over Processes and Tools

People and communication are more important than depending only on tools and processes.

### 2. Working Software Over Comprehensive Documentation

A working product is more valuable than creating large amounts of documentation.

### 3. Customer Collaboration Over Contract Negotiation

Developers should continuously work with customers and understand their requirements.

### 4. Responding to Change Over Following a Plan

Requirements can change during development, so the team should adapt to changes.

> **Important:**
> The items on the right are still valuable, but Agile gives **more importance to the items on the left**.

---

## 1.3 12 Agile Principles

### 1. Customer Satisfaction

The highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.

### 2. Welcome Changing Requirements

Changes in requirements should be accepted, even during later stages of development.

### 3. Frequent Delivery

Working software should be delivered frequently in short intervals.

### 4. Collaboration

Business people and developers should work together regularly.

### 5. Motivated Individuals

Teams should consist of motivated and responsible people and should be given proper support.

### 6. Face-to-Face Communication

Direct communication is considered an effective way of sharing information.

### 7. Working Software

Working software is the main measure of progress.

### 8. Sustainable Development

The development team should maintain a sustainable pace of work.

### 9. Technical Excellence

Continuous attention to good design and technical quality improves agility.

### 10. Simplicity

The team should avoid unnecessary work and focus on essential requirements.

### 11. Self-Organizing Teams

The best solutions often come from self-organizing and empowered teams.

### 12. Continuous Improvement

The team should regularly reflect on its performance and improve its processes.

---

## 1.4 Advantages of Agile

* Faster software delivery
* High customer involvement
* Easy to handle changing requirements
* Frequent feedback
* Better communication
* Continuous improvement
* Early identification of problems
* Better product quality
* Reduced development risk

### Disadvantages of Agile

* Difficult to predict exact cost and completion time
* Requires highly skilled and responsible team members
* Frequent changes can increase project scope
* Less suitable when requirements must remain fixed
* Continuous customer involvement may be difficult
* Documentation may receive less attention
* Poor planning can cause project problems

# 2. Scrum Framework

**Scrum** is an Agile framework used to develop, deliver, and maintain complex software products.

Scrum divides development into small iterations called **Sprints**.

A Sprint generally has a **fixed duration** and results in a potentially usable **Product Increment**.

### Scrum has three major areas

1. **Scrum Roles / Accountabilities**
2. **Scrum Events**
3. **Scrum Artifacts**

---

## 2.1 Scrum Roles

The modern Scrum framework describes the Scrum Team through **three accountabilities**:

### 1. Product Owner

The **Product Owner** is responsible for **maximizing the value of the product**.

#### Main Responsibilities

* Manages and orders the Product Backlog
* Understands customer requirements
* Defines Product Goals
* Prioritizes features
* Communicates with stakeholders

---

### 2. Scrum Master

The **Scrum Master** helps everyone understand and follow Scrum.

#### Responsibilities

* Facilitates Scrum events
* Removes or helps resolve obstacles
* Supports the Scrum Team
* Helps improve Scrum practices
* Ensures effective communication

---

### 3. Developers

**Developers** are the people who create the Product Increment.

#### Responsibilities

* Plan the work
* Develop features
* Test the software
* Fix defects
* Maintain quality
* Deliver a usable increment

---

# 2.2 Scrum Events

Scrum events provide opportunities for **inspection and adaptation**.

### 1. Sprint

A **Sprint** is a fixed-length period during which the team works to create a usable Product Increment.

---

### 2. Sprint Planning

The team decides:

* **What** work should be done?
* **Why** is the work valuable?
* **How** will the selected work be completed?

---

### 3. Daily Scrum

A short daily meeting where Developers inspect progress toward the **Sprint Goal** and adjust their plan.

---

### 4. Sprint Review

At the end of the Sprint, the team reviews the completed work with stakeholders and discusses future changes.

---

### 5. Sprint Retrospective

The team discusses:

* What went well?
* What problems occurred?
* What can be improved?

---

# 2.3 Scrum Artifacts

There are **three main Scrum artifacts**:

### 1. Product Backlog

A prioritized list of work required to improve the product.

### 2. Sprint Backlog

The selected Product Backlog items for the Sprint along with the plan for delivering them.

### 3. Increment

The usable, completed product functionality produced during a Sprint.

### Easy Way to Remember

| Artifact            | Meaning                    |
| ------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Product Backlog** | What can be done           |
| **Sprint Backlog**  | What we will do now        |
| **Increment**       | What we actually completed |

---

# 3. Product Backlog

## 3.1 Definition

A **Product Backlog** is an ordered list of everything that may be needed to improve a product.

It is managed and ordered by the **Product Owner**.

The Product Backlog is continuously updated according to:

* Customer requirements
* Business needs
* Feedback
* Market changes
* Technical improvements

---

## 3.2 Purpose of Product Backlog

The Product Backlog helps the team:

* Understand product requirements
* Prioritize important work
* Plan future Sprints
* Maintain transparency
* Respond to changing requirements
* Deliver maximum business value

---

## 3.3 Components of Product Backlog

A Product Backlog may contain:

* User Stories
* Features
* Bug fixes
* Technical improvements
* Research tasks
* Non-functional requirements
* Enhancements

### Example

For an **Online Shopping Website**:

| Priority  | Backlog Item    |
| --------- | --------------- |
| 🔴 High   | User Login      |
| 🔴 High   | Product Search  |
| 🔴 High   | Payment         |
| 🟡 Medium | Wishlist        |
| 🟡 Medium | Product Reviews |
| 🟢 Low    | Dark Mode       |

---

## 3.4 Product Backlog Prioritization

**Prioritization** means arranging backlog items according to their importance and value.

### Common Factors

* Business value
* Customer importance
* Urgency
* Risk
* Dependencies
* Development effort

### Example

If the **Payment System** is essential for launching an online shopping website, it may have a higher priority than **Dark Mode**.

> **Remember:** The **Product Owner** is responsible for ordering the Product Backlog.

# 4. Sprint Planning

## 4.1 What is Sprint Planning?

**Sprint Planning** is a Scrum event conducted at the beginning of every Sprint.

During Sprint Planning, the Scrum Team decides:

> **What should be completed in the Sprint and how will it be done?**

The result is the **Sprint Goal** and **Sprint Backlog**.

---

## 4.2 Inputs of Sprint Planning

Important inputs include:

* Product Backlog
* Product Goal
* Previous Sprint performance
* Team capacity
* Past experience
* Product information
* Stakeholder/customer requirements

---

## 4.3 Activities of Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning mainly involves the following steps:

### Step 1: Define Sprint Goal

The team determines the main objective of the Sprint.

### Step 2: Select Product Backlog Items

The team selects suitable high-priority items from the Product Backlog.

### Step 3: Estimate Work

Developers estimate the amount of effort required.

### Step 4: Create Implementation Plan

The team decides how the selected work will be completed.

### Step 5: Create Sprint Backlog

The selected items and the plan for delivering them form the **Sprint Backlog**.

---

## 4.4 Outputs of Sprint Planning

The major outputs are:

### 1. Sprint Goal

A clear objective that the Sprint aims to achieve.

### 2. Sprint Backlog

Selected Product Backlog items plus the Developers' plan.

### 3. Initial Plan for Delivery

The team has an initial understanding of how the work will be completed.

---

# 5. Sprint Backlog

## 5.1 Definition

A **Sprint Backlog** is the collection of Product Backlog items selected for a particular Sprint, together with the plan for delivering them.

It is created during **Sprint Planning**.

The Sprint Backlog is primarily managed by the **Developers**.

---

## 5.2 Purpose of Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog helps the team:

* Focus on Sprint Goals
* Track Sprint work
* Organize daily activities
* Understand what needs to be completed
* Monitor progress
* Adapt the plan during the Sprint

---

## 5.3 Product Backlog vs Sprint Backlog

| Product Backlog                     | Sprint Backlog                                     |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Contains all potential product work | Contains selected work for a Sprint                |
| Product-level view                  | Sprint-level view                                  |
| Ordered by Product Owner            | Managed by Developers                              |
| Continuously updated                | Changes as Developers learn more during the Sprint |
| Used for overall product planning   | Used for Sprint execution                          |
| Contains future and current work    | Focuses on the current Sprint                      |

---

## Simple Example

### Product Backlog

```text
Login → Search → Cart → Payment → Reviews → Wishlist → Dark Mode
```

### Sprint Backlog — Sprint 1

```text
Login → Search → Cart
```

Therefore:

> **Product Backlog = Complete list of potential work**

> **Sprint Backlog = Selected work for the current Sprint**
